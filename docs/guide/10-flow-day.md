## Morning: what's due

You don't need to plan anything. Tide has already planned the week (see [Your week](21-week.md)) and Home shows today's slice of it. The first item is almost always **Daily practice**, a short mixed session of everything that's due:

- flashcards, overdue and most-forgotten first, plus up to five new ones,
- retests of past mistakes from your mistake journal,
- explaining a note from memory,
- a first look at the next topic in your plan.

They're mixed so the same note never comes twice in a row, because switching between topics is what makes recall stick. Written answers are marked in a few seconds. Keys: <kbd>Space</kbd> shows a card's answer, <kbd>1</kbd>–<kbd>4</kbd> rate it, <kbd>⌘↵</kbd> checks a written answer and moves on. It ends on what's next in your plan, with a button to start that lesson.

## Midday: learning something new

Click the next topic on your timeline. The lesson opens with the pages to read at the top if you own a book that covers it, then teaches it. Answer with a confidence (**Guessing**, **Unsure** or **Sure**); being confidently wrong counts as a misconception and comes back as a retest.

A step is done after two right answers in a row without hints, and later steps mix in a problem from earlier ones. The hint button climbs a ladder (Nudge, Hint, Show a step, Show the answer), so you only take as much help as you need. When you're wrong, the tutor points at the exact step that fails before anything else.

## Breaks, without a timer

Tide logs study time on its own while you're actually working: typing, scrolling, the tutor replying. Three quiet minutes stop the clock. After about 25 minutes of lessons (45 of reading or coding) the tutor offers a break; during it he goes fishing, in the menu bar too, and a chime says when it's over. Exams and timed interviews are never interrupted. Change the pace in **Settings → Study time**.

If you prefer a fixed session, **Focus** at the bottom of the sidebar times one (25, 50 or 90 minutes).

## Evening: wrap up

**Wrap up the day** takes about three minutes: a sentence on what you did, three quick questions on today's topics, moving what's left to your next study day (in Things too), and a line for your journal in `Coach/Journal`.

## If you don't finish

Nothing is lost. Whatever you didn't finish moves to the top of your next study day by itself, in Tide and in Things, the next time Tide opens or within the hour, even if you skip the wrap-up. Anything you ticked off in Things counts as done first, so finished work never moves. If the next study day falls in a new week, the next week's plan fits it in first. A week you don't finish also makes the next one lighter.


At the end of the week, the weekly review shows each day's activity and asks what went well, what got in the way and what to change, then plans the next week with that in mind.
