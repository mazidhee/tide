## How the plan is made

Planning runs in two steps.

1. **Sizing.** Tide reads the web for how long the subject takes and how it's taught, and decides its size. A single tool like Metabase or Docker gets a few weeks; a skill a few months; a field like stablecoins and payments infrastructure most of a year; a whole career two years or more. It adjusts for what you already know, your **hours a week**, and a **finish by** date if you give one, and says so if that date is unrealistic.
2. **The roadmap.** One part per phase, each with its dates, chapters of topics with the best book, course or docs for each, two or three practice exercises, a project and an exam on the phase's last day.

Before anything is saved you see the length, why, a timeline of the phases and what's included. **Create notebook** then saves the notebook with its dated roadmap, a **Reading list** note, a goal with the finish date and, for programming subjects, the exercises as coding challenges in Practice.

When you've already got a direction (for example low-level software engineering for payments and data), the plan leans that way. **Overview**, **Course** and **Deep** still draft an undated outline of about 25, 70 or 150 topics if that's all you want.

## Working in the outline

The notebook's **Outline** tab is the syllabus, saved as a plain Markdown checklist so it also works in Obsidian. Each topic links to its note and shows how well you know it. Hover a topic and choose **Note** to create its note in the right chapter folder.

```markdown
## Part I — Numbers
### Chapter 1: Numbers
- [x] [[2. The Invention of Zero|The invention of zero]]
- [ ] Prime numbers
```

**Audit** reviews a roadmap frankly: how long it really is at your pace, prerequisites that come too late, gaps, what to cut, and a concrete plan for the next four weeks.

## Staying on pace

The goal card on Home says, in one line, when you'll finish at the pace you've actually kept: “At your 3 h a week you'd finish 19 Jan, 12 days late. About 4.3 h a week gets you there.” Your pace is the study time Tide logged on that notebook over the last three weeks. When you're late, **Make it fit** helps you choose between more hours, a later date, or which topics to cut or skim.
