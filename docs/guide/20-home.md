## The timeline

Your day runs down the page: what you've finished (with the time), **Now**, then the rest of today and this evening. It reads Today in Things 4, but only the tasks Tide gave you (tagged **tide**): your plan's items and anything the tutor set. Your own tasks, work included, stay in Things and don't show here. A planned item that's also a Things to-do shows once. Things with a reminder time come first, in order. The timeline checks Things every minute and when you come back to the window, so a task the tutor just set slides in.

Ask the tutor to plan your day (“put a 45-minute stablecoins lesson and the take-home on today”) and it sets the tasks in Things for today.

## The cards below

**This week**, **Goals** and **Needs another look** sit in a row under your day at every window size, so the day always reads first.

- **This week:** study time per day and your streak. **Your week** opens the plan.
- **Goals:** a deadline on a roadmap, how far along you are, whether you're on pace, and the next topic. **Make it fit** when you're behind.
- **Needs another look:** notes whose recent answers went badly, each with **Quiz**.

## Daily practice

**Daily practice** gathers everything due today into one short session: flashcards, retests of past mistakes, explaining a note from memory, and a first look at the next topic. Written answers are marked in a few seconds, mistakes update their retest streak, and it ends on what's next in your plan.

## Fresh versions of past mistakes

A retest in Daily practice doesn't repeat the question you got wrong: it asks a new problem on the same idea (different numbers and situation), written and checked ahead of time, so it tests whether you understand it rather than whether you remember the answer. The first look at your next topic is a real first problem from the bank when one is ready.

## The mistake journal

Every answer you get wrong, and every mark you lose in an exam, goes into `Coach/Mistakes` with what you said and the right idea. Each comes back as a quick retest the next day, then in three days; two right answers on different days puts it right. Retests sit at the top of your day.

## Wrapping up

**Wrap up the day** is four short steps: the day in a sentence, three quick questions, moving what's left to your next study day, and a line for your journal. The weekly review looks back on the week and plans the next one.

## If you don't finish

Nothing is lost. Whatever you didn't finish moves to the top of your next study day by itself, in Tide and in Things, the next time Tide opens or within the hour, even if you skip the wrap-up. Anything you ticked off in Things counts as done first, so finished work never moves. If the next study day falls in a new week, the next week's plan fits it in first. A week you don't finish also makes the next one lighter.
