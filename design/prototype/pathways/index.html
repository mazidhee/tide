    The two forms

    Flat

    Concepts run left to right in the order they can be learnt: a concept sits one step to the right of the deepest thing it rests on. Areas are bands. The frontier is the right-hand edge of what is filled in, and the ringed concepts on it are ready next.

    Best for daily use: every label is readable, nothing hides behind anything else, the same concept is always in the same place, and it costs nothing when you are not touching it. Drawn on a 2D canvas.

    Depth

    Each area is a tower on a ring, fundamentals at the foot, deeper concepts higher up. Turning it shows how the areas lean on each other; the links between towers are the cross-subject pathways.

    Best for seeing the whole shape at once and for the links between subjects. Costs: things hide behind each other, labels must be culled harder, every pathway is one pixel wide (WebGL lines), and it needs the GPU while you move it.

    What 3D costs on this Mac

    Gemma runs on the same GPU. So both forms draw only when something changes: when you are not touching the map, nothing is drawn, and the GPU and CPU fall idle. Depth also asks for the low-power GPU path, caps its resolution at 1.5x, drops to 1x while a model is working, and skips the fly-to animation when Reduce motion is on.

    Measured on this M4 Pro in headless Chrome (ANGLE on Metal), 1440 by 900 at 2x, no model running: both forms hold 60 frames a second while moving at 320 and at 2,000 concepts. A flat frame takes 0.6 ms (320) or 1.3 ms (2,000) of script; a depth frame 0.3 to 0.4 ms of script and about 1.2 ms with the GPU waited on. Left alone, both draw no frames at all and use under 0.1 ms of CPU a second. Open with ?fps=1 to watch the counter.
